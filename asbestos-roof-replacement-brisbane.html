Do You Have Asbestos?

Signs Your Roof May Contain Asbestos

Queensland homes built before the late 1980s were commonly roofed with asbestos cement sheeting — usually corrugated “Super Six” fibro. Visual identification alone is not reliable; the only definitive confirmation is laboratory testing by a licensed asbestos assessor. These indicators suggest asbestos may be present:

Built or Roofed Before 1986

Homes built or last re-roofed before 1986 have a high probability of asbestos cement roofing.

Fibre Cement Texture

Corrugated or flat sheeting with a dull grey, non-metallic fibre cement look rather than steel.

Thick Sheets, Smooth Edges

Sheeting approximately 6mm thick with smooth edges is characteristic of Super Six asbestos cement.

Known as “Fibro”

Previous owners or neighbours describing the roof as fibro is a strong indicator worth testing.

How It Works

The Legally Compliant Removal & Replacement Process

Queensland law is strict: bonded asbestos roofing must be removed by a licensed Class-B removalist, disposed of at an approved EPA facility, and cleared by a licensed assessor before new roofing goes on. We manage every step under a single contract.

01

Asbestos Assessment & Report

A licensed asbestos assessor takes material samples and provides a written report confirming the presence and condition of asbestos, alongside our fully itemised quote.

02

Licensed Removal & EPA Disposal

A Class-B licensed, EPA-registered removalist we coordinate removes all sheeting using wet suppression and containment, then double-bags, labels, and disposes of it at an approved facility — with documentation.

03

Clearance & Structural Upgrade

A licensed assessor issues a formal clearance certificate before further work. We then inspect the timber frame and upgrade battens and tie-downs to current cyclone-rated QLD standards.

04

Colorbond® Installation & Finish

Genuine BlueScope® Colorbond® sheeting, ridge capping, new gutters and downpipes with Anticon® insulation as standard — followed by a quality walk-through and full clean-up.

What You Need to Know

Asbestos Roofing in Queensland: The Essentials

What Super Six is, what the law requires, and exactly what you receive when the job is done properly.

⚠️

What Is Super Six (Fibro) Asbestos Roofing?

Queensland homes built or roofed before the mid-1980s commonly used asbestos cement (“Super Six” fibro) sheeting. It looks like corrugated or flat fibre cement — dull grey, not metallic, around 6mm thick with smooth edges. It can be easily confused with modern non-asbestos fibre cement and old corrugated iron.

Visual identification alone is not reliable. The only definitive confirmation is laboratory testing of a sample taken by a licensed asbestos assessor. Read our full asbestos roof removal guide for how to identify it.

⚖️

What Queensland Law Requires

Homeowners must not remove, cut, drill, repair, pressure-wash, sand, or otherwise disturb their own asbestos roofing — even on their own property. All bonded asbestos roofing must be removed by a licensed Class-B asbestos removalist.

The waste must be double-bagged, labelled, and disposed of at an approved EPA facility — it cannot go into a standard skip bin. After removal, a licensed assessor must issue a clearance certificate before any new roofing is installed. These are legal requirements, not options.
